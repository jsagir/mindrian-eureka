# Algorithm origins and why each service exists

**Scope:** current Mindrian Eureka capability catalog, version 2026-10-20+standalone-graph-algorithm-services-host-handoff-capability-chains. This document explains the source lineage and intended use of every catalog entry. “Why” is the service's job-to-be-done, not proof that its method works.

## How to read the origins

- **Verified local origin** means a source path, revision, hash, or implementation is recorded in this repository or its source ledger. It does not establish ownership or redistribution rights.
- **Theoretical lineage** is separate from the implementation lineage. A known idea may inspire a local method without validating that method as a faithful implementation.
- **Unresolved** means the provided files do not establish original authorship, publication, or the creator's rationale. The catalog's job statement describes why Mindrian exposes the method; it must not be mistaken for the source author's stated reason.
- The implementation field in the catalog identifies the current code entrypoint. The source ledger identifies source files, hashes, rights, adaptations, and evaluation limits.

## Research lineages confirmed from primary sources

- **Two different “RS” usages must stay separate.** The method behind `opportunity.find_cross_domain_pairs` is called “Algorithmic Reverse Salient” in its local method guide, but that guide explicitly says it is a similarity-disagreement opportunity method, **not** Hughes's reverse-salient concept. Its source lineage is a user-provided algorithm-analysis/deck and LATEST RS code; the original deck/source package is not in this checkout, so authorship, exact fidelity, and the source author's rationale remain unresolved. Its Mindrian service purpose is to find unusual structural/semantic pair differences for investigation. By contrast, bottleneck/reverse-salient services such as `portfolio.discover_rs_whitespace` build on the system-evolution idea in Thomas P. Hughes's [*Networks of Power*](https://www.press.jhu.edu/books/title/2031/networks-power): uneven system growth can leave a critical component lagging and constrain the whole system. These distinct meanings share an acronym; do not transfer one method's origin to the other.
- **SAPPhIRE:** Chakrabarti, Sarkar, Leelavathamma, and Nataraju's 2005 paper, [“A functional representation for aiding biomimetic and artificial inspiration of new ideas”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0890060405050109), introduced a causal representation of natural and engineered systems to support systematic inspiration. That motivates the analogy services' structured source-to-target comparison; the current local retrieval and fitness scores are adaptations and do not prove transferability.
- **Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP):** Saaty's work describes pairwise judgments and consistency-checked priorities for multi-criteria decisions ([Saaty, 1987](https://doi.org/10.1016/0270-0255%2887%2990473-8)). The portfolio-dimensions service uses an AHP-weighted signal to make explicit criteria and inconsistent judgments inspectable. This is a research signal, not a calibrated opportunity verdict.
- **Bayesian surprise:** Itti and Baldi define surprise as the change from prior to posterior belief and study its relationship to attention ([NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2005/file/0172d289da48c48de8c5ebf3de9f7ee1-Paper.pdf); [expanded treatment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2860069/)). Eureka's graph method only scores caller-supplied prior/posterior probabilities; the conceptual lineage does not mean the service reproduces their attention model.
- **Standalone graph-algorithm lineages:** These academic methods explain the component algorithms, not authorship of the user-provided LATEST graph-relations package. Open-triad ranking uses shared-neighbor measures including Adamic–Adar, introduced for mining Web relationships by [Adamic and Adar (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0378-8733(03)00009-1). Community labels and node bridge scores draw on label propagation ([Raghavan, Albert, and Kumara (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.76.036106)), Brandes betweenness ([Brandes (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1080/0022250X.2001.9990249)), and participation coefficients from network functional cartography ([Guimerà and Amaral (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature03288)). The category-atypicality motivation is related to [Uzzi et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1240474), but this implementation's rewired graph-category z-scores are not their paper-level novelty/impact measure. Text-gap retrieval uses a local TF-IDF cosine implementation, conceptually related to the vector-space information-retrieval tradition ([Salton, Wong, and Yang (1975)](https://doi.org/10.1145/361219.361220)); it is lexical similarity, not semantic understanding. The induced-four-cycle detector is a repository-port of a LATEST graph-relations method; induced cycles are graph-theoretic structures, while the optional Ripser output is a separate persistent-homology diagnostic ([Ripser paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02518)). These connections explain why the instruments exist; they do not validate them for MindrianOS discovery.

Other families originate in user-provided LATEST or dated research packages, existing MindrianOS code, or repository-authored adapters. Where those materials do not cite a paper or explain author intent, the service table says so rather than assigning an external origin.
