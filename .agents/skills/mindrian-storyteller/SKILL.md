# Mindrian storyteller

One honest core, told to anyone, then tested. Two modes: Tell and Test. Use both before anything leaves the seat.

## Before you start

1. Run `npx --no-install mos-brand doctor`, then `npx --no-install mos-brand context`. Read `foundations/master-story`, `foundations/positioning` and `writing/voice` from the paths it returns.
2. Say who the listener is. If you cannot, ask one question. Then pick the closest row in `references/audiences.md`.
3. Never invent a number, result, customer, quote or capability. Missing proof goes in a missing-material list.

## Tell

Follow the presenter canon order: a familiar situation, a question that makes it interesting, one concrete example, one useful distinction, a next move the listener can act on.

- Keep the core fixed. Change only vocabulary, analogy, order, depth and the kind of proof.
- Show the quest honestly: the target can move, and the route is found through work.
- Keep problem, solution and business case as three connected questions, never three rooms done in order.
- An analogy is an illustration. Label it so, and never let it stand in for a fact.
- Plain words first. Name the idea before the method. Hyphens only; never use an em dash.

## Test

Run these four checks on your own story before handing it over:

1. Assumptions: what does this take for granted that may not hold?
2. Evidence: is it the right proof, and is it current?
3. Logic: does the ending follow from the middle?
4. Conclusions: is the claim bigger than the evidence?

Then offer one rival reading: "the evidence might also support Y". Offers are hedged, never asserted. Check the listener row again: would this person understand it on the first read? See `references/critical-test.md`.

## Hand back

1. The story, ready to use.
2. What would break it: the two or three weakest points, each with the evidence that would settle it.
3. Missing material: proof, quotes, numbers and results you did not invent.
4. Current versus planned: label every capability.

A person approves before anything publishes. Terminal output uses the installed mindrian-tui-designer skill. Written pieces also use mindrian-draft-content.
