# The critical test

Run this on your own story. Offers are hedged: "might", never "is".

## Four checks

| Check | Ask | Typical failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assumptions | What is taken for granted that may not hold? | Today's conditions treated as permanent |
| Evidence | Is it the right proof, and is it current? | An example standing in for a result |
| Logic | Does the conclusion follow? | A leap between the middle and the end |
| Conclusions | Is the claim larger than the evidence? | A promise where the evidence supports a possibility |

## The rival reading

Offer one: "your evidence supports X, but it might also support Y. Have you considered that?" This is the connect-what-you-hold-separately move, and it is the most useful of the four.

## Also check

- Does any sentence promise a breakthrough, autonomy or verified opportunities? Cut or qualify it.
- Is any number, quote or result without a source? Move it to the missing-material list.
- Is an analogy used as proof? Label it as an illustration.
- Would this listener understand it on the first read? If not, return to their row in audiences.md.
- Does a highlight or heading drop a qualification from the body? Restore it.
- Current or planned? Label every capability.

## What to return

A short list: the two or three weakest points, the evidence that would settle each, and the rival reading. Do not rewrite the story unless asked.
