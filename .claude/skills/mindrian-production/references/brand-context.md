# Inherited brand rules

1. Never use em dashes. This blocks release in every language and channel.
2. Preserve the exact face / m:os parent logo in LTR order.
3. MindrianOS leads; the DataRoom is the developing project; Theo supports the inquiry.
4. The person retains judgment. Do not promise autonomous strategy or guaranteed breakthroughs.
5. Keep facts, assumptions, proposed connections and results distinct.
6. Use the bundled fonts, shared tokens and meaningful movement.
7. Start from the job and return learning to the project.
8. Family identities remain distinct while inheriting these rules.
9. Family specimens and untested behavior remain labelled.
10. Keep one canonical artifact home; exports and indexes are generated.

11. Mindrian branded generated speech uses the owner-selected ElevenLabs voice in the [spoken identity](../segments/identity/spoken-voice/ROOM.md). Family services inherit it; never substitute another voice automatically.

12. Homepage and comparable long-form project scrollytelling follows the [continuous project story directive](../segments/experience/scroll/entries/2026-10-19/SKILL.md). It supersedes conflicting older choreography and fixed-frame prescriptions within that scope.

13. Critical: no walls of text. Substantial content must be scannable and easy to read through meaningful hierarchy, manageable paragraphs, selective rich-text emphasis and useful visual explanation. Keep qualifications and actions visible. Dense undifferentiated copy or indiscriminate formatting blocks editorial handoff. Apply the [text augmentation procedure](../segments/writing/text-augmentation/entries/2026-10-19/SKILL.md); human review is required, and passing source checks alone does not satisfy it.

14. Explain the quest as navigating uncertainty and changing conditions. Avoid treating today’s needs, technologies or constraints as permanent limits. Keep possible futures conditional and the next move practical. Apply the [quest guidance](../segments/foundations/quest/entries/2026-10-20/GUIDE.md).

15. Highlighting never upgrades evidence status. Direct quotes require exact wording, context and visible attribution. Trace consequential factual claims and technical instructions to inspected support; preserve qualifications across channels. Apply the [editorial integrity ruling](../segments/writing/editorial-integrity/entries/2026-10-20/SKILL.md).

16. Required before substantial content drafting or revision: apply the installed Mindrian drafting skill and actual selected brand instructions, prepare the reader brief and scan outline, then review the brief before prose. Use the [content production ruling](../skills/draft-content/SKILL.md) and, for articles, the [blog production pipeline](../segments/teams/editorial/blog/pipeline/entries/2026-10-20/GUIDE.md). A managed content run must have current preparation before advancing. Registration is not proof of factual truth, completed human review or publication authorization.

17. Before creating blogs, generated images, visual aids or animation, follow the [project tools and rendering preflight](../segments/governance/tooling/production/entries/2026-10-20/GUIDE.md). Inspect real capabilities, install supported missing skills/dependencies and apply the actual selected instructions. Keep unavailable providers and unknown external installers explicit. Choose rendering from the explanation: static guides are allowed, but required interaction or motion must not be removed because a matching component is unavailable.

18. Graphs, 2×2 matrices, pyramids, systems and framework explanations follow the [visual explanation grammar](../segments/experience/visual-language/entries/2026-10-20/GUIDE.md) and their matching child recipe. Choose the encoding before the appearance. Carry Theo Faces thinking gestures into the relationship, without forcing a face into every figure. Keep actual values, proposed connections, attention, method execution and validation distinct; settle into a complete readable still.

19. Every animated Shaper component follows the [Shaper motion ruling](../segments/experience/visual-language/shaper/motion/entries/2026-10-20/GUIDE.md). Declare relevance and teaching purpose, use the shared bounded player or governed scroll driver, and keep the complete still. Independent ambient loops or page-specific competing clocks are not allowed. New components require a motion contract before animation.

20. Critical: geometry follows meaning. Brand identity and React do not mandate square nodes, straight paths, right angles or sharp corners in explanatory visuals. Preserve circular, curved, organic, irregular or mixed forms when the relationship needs them, and keep straight scales or hard boundaries where meaningful. Apply the parent visual-language geometry ruling before styling or motion.

21. New CSS capabilities are enhancements only. Follow the [CSS capabilities room](../segments/experience/css-capabilities/entries/2026-10-23/GUIDE.md): a full semantic base that works without the capability, an explicit enhancement boundary with a feature test and an off switch, and a tested fallback. Support claims carry a date and a source; an experimental browser feature never carries essential content. The room is active guidance. Its scoped compliance ruling (css-capabilities-ruling) stays proposed until the owner rules, and only the first-wave capabilities (interpolate-size and gap decorations) may ship as enhancements; the other eight stay lab, prototype, tracking or required-now.


## segments/experience/css-capabilities/entries/2026-10-23/GUIDE.md

# CSS capabilities

Status: ACTIVE guidance since 2026-10-23, by the owner's delegated decision, because the base (enhancement off) path of all three examples passed in every engine that could be run. The scoped compliance ruling (css-capabilities-ruling) stays PROPOSED. This room is a staged lane for new CSS capabilities. It does not replace the brand language, adopt a whole article as a package or remove any current production route.

Basis: the owner brief for the brand-book manager, written against book release 2.41.0 (commit 202bf6e7e6598dac3cbe1183d6eed4afdb0f8643, 2026-10-21). It was adapted on the 2.43.0 branch. Support was checked on 2026-10-23 (see Verification record). Only the first wave, interpolate-size and gap decorations, may ship as enhancements; the other eight stay lab, prototype, tracking or required-now.

## What this lane is for

Start with cases that make evidence, a relationship or a change of state clearer: evidence opened in place, a source kept tied to its claim, and a clear structural division of a group. An experimental capability enters only as an enhancement over a base that already works. Functions, experimental geometry and inherit() stay in a lab or tracking lane until a need is proven and support is verified.

## The canon stays in force

- Geometry follows meaning (brand rule 20). Do not make rectangles mandatory, and do not add morphing to make a page feel like AI. The identity of an entity holds across states.
- One explanation shows one meaningful change, grounds its context, explains the relation or action and settles in a readable state. Motion creates no evidence or decision that the data does not hold.
- The static state is enough to understand the message. Content, links and actions work without the animation, without the new capability and under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce.
- Reversible scroll suits a relation or a process when the reader controls progress. It is not a rule for every component. A visual reversal does not change an evidence status and does not imitate human approval. Evidence stays available on an accessible reading path.

## The rule for every enhancement

1. Build the full semantic base first.
2. Put the enhancement behind a feature test (@supports) and an explicit off switch.
3. Keep the same facts, labels, source status and actions in both paths.
4. Name the fallback and test it.
5. Gate any transition by reduced motion, and gate the transition itself so an engine without support has no delay.
6. Record what was tested. A syntax test checks syntax, not behavior.

## The ten capabilities

The machine-readable record is capabilities.json. Each of the ten has a meaningful role, an anti-pattern, a syntax source, a test date, a maturity, a use decision, feature detection, a fallback, a reduced-motion line and a required test.

| # | Capability | Use decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | @function | lab |
| 2 | corner-shape | lab |
| 3 | border-shape | lab |
| 4 | Anchored container queries | prototype (source beside a claim) |
| 5 | inherit() | track only |
| 6 | Scroll-triggered animations | prototype (controlled story) |
| 7 | reading-flow and reading-order | require correct DOM order now |
| 8 | interpolate-size and calc-size() | adopt first (first wave), over an immediate disclosure |
| 9 | Gap decorations | adopt first (first wave), over ordinary borders |
| 10 | Scroll-state queries | adopt later, for orientation |

Outside the ten: basic anchoring with popover can support a source attached to a claim, and same-document View Transitions can keep identity from a card to its detail. Neither commits the book to the wider API family or to cross-document transitions.

Support statements in capabilities.json were checked on 2026-10-23 against MDN browser-compat-data (main branch), MDN and CSSWG pages, and local engine tests; each carries its URL and date. The article lists candidates for Interop 2027, not a promise from any browser vendor. Re-check before any later ruling. A brand procedure never turns an experimental browser feature into a stable one.

## Verification record (2026-10-23)

- Sources: MDN browser-compat-data on the main branch (raw JSON per feature), the MDN @function and corner-shape pages, the CSSWG css-anchor-position-2 and css-values-5 drafts. The MDN pages fetched through the reader gave syntax and status wording but no compatibility table, so per-engine versions come from browser-compat-data.
- Engines run: Chromium 145.0.7632.0, Chromium 149.0.7827.0 and Firefox 146.0.1 (Playwright builds). WebKit downloaded but could not launch (missing system libraries on this host). iOS Safari and Android Chrome are pending human cells owned by the owner.
- Findings: the brief's Chromium-only claims match browser-compat-data for ten of ten, with new detail (for example row-rule starts at Chrome 149, so Chromium 145 shows the border fallback; Firefox 146 supports none of the first-wave enhancements and shows the base path). The from-memory anchored syntax (container-type: anchored; @container anchored(fallback: ...)) matches the CSSWG draft. inherit() syntax is confirmed as inherit(<custom-property-name>, <declaration-value>?); no engine tested supports it.
- Results of the example runs are in acceptance.md.

## Corrections the brief makes to its source article

- inherit() reads the computed value of a custom property on the parent: inherit(--custom-property, fallback). It does not read ordinary properties. Do not copy an empty call.
- animation-trigger (scroll-triggered) starts a time-based sequence at a threshold. It is not scroll-driven progress. The two are separate features.
- A gap-decoration separator tells records apart. It does not draw a relation that the data lacks.
- Anchored container queries style children of the anchored container, not the container.

## Three reference examples

They live in examples/ and share css-capabilities.css. Each page has the same content and labels in both paths and a button that turns the enhancement off. They are proposals for implementation and testing, not approved components.

1. evidence-in-place.html: a details element with a clear summary. The source and status are inside the content. It opens at once without the enhancement. With interpolate-size the panel animates, with no guessed max-height that could cut Hebrew or a translation. It uses the approved tokens: 200ms feedback (--mos-time-feedback) and the --mos-ease curve. The feature gate tests syntax only, so test the whole combination: selector, content-visibility, transition-behavior, repeated open and close, and keyboard. Keep all evidence readable in print and static export (the export builder opens the details element; the print rule in the stylesheet helps only where supported).
2. source-tied-to-claim.html: a popover opened from a named button. Without popover support the source shows in the flow. Basic anchoring improves placement. An anchored container query can turn the pointer inside the panel when a fallback position is chosen. Test all four screen edges, close, focus return and RTL. A source never opens on hover alone. The anchored query syntax matches the CSSWG draft and ran in Chromium 145 and 149 (the pointer flipped at the bottom edges); it is absent in Firefox 146, where the popover keeps working without a pointer.
3. group-boundary.html: the same labelled members in a plain bordered group and with an optional gap-decoration separator. The border and the list state membership. A separator distinguishes records and does not make them related. Do not change the logo or every card to show off an API. A corner or shell change is allowed only if it clarifies an assigned role. The border-shape variant stays in the lab until it is written and tested.

## Reversible scroll-driven reference (lab)

This is scroll-driven, separate from the scroll-triggered capability in the list. The relation mark is a visual mark inside an explanation area. The text beside it always states the relation and does not depend on the mark. Mark only the mark as aria-hidden="true". Do not read the line as execution progress or as verified evidence.

```css
.explanation { view-timeline: --explain block; }
.relation-mark {
  display: block;
  transform: scaleX(1); /* complete static state */
  transform-origin: left center;
}
.relation-mark:dir(rtl) { transform-origin: right center; }
@keyframes explain-relation {
  from { transform: scaleX(0); }
  to   { transform: scaleX(1); }
}
@supports (animation-timeline: --explain) and
          (view-timeline: --explain block) and
          (animation-range: entry 0% cover 60%) {
  @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference) {
    .relation-mark {
      animation: explain-relation 1ms linear both;
      animation-timeline: --explain;
      animation-range: entry 0% cover 60%;
    }
  }
}
@media print { .relation-mark { animation: none !important; } }
```

Put a mark with a visible line inside the container that defines the timeline. The same scroll position must give the same state going forward and back. Write animation-timeline after the animation shorthand so the shorthand does not reset it. Scroll-driven support is not guaranteed in every browser; verify the engines and versions the product needs.

## Production rule

A CSS renderer with motion needs the contract in the production preflight: purpose, relevance, geometry meaning, feature ids from capabilities.json, support source and date, fallback, still, replay, loop policy, reduced motion and test targets. A missing field or an unknown capability id fails. Native scroll in CSS needs its own adapter and renderer contract; until then it is lab only. React and GSAP are not replaced. Every property and every playhead has one owner.

- The device cells (iOS Safari, Android Chrome) and the WebKit engine are still to be run.

# Required Google login, then an optional introduction

Status: ACTIVE brand directive, owner-approved on 20 October 2026. Website implementation and security verification remain pending. This replaces the previous introduction-only access direction.

## Scope

Google sign-in applies only where the product needs a signed-in identity: the account page, the Theo connection consent and letter preferences. Blog index, articles, installation pages and documentation are open and need no sign-in. Keep the homepage, product explanation, privacy information and Brand Manager reading site open too. Do not force this login policy onto unrelated family services. For a project outside this scope, record non-applicability rather than adding authentication because it uses the brand.

Narrowed on 2026-10-21 by owner ruling: reading and install stay open. The earlier text of 2026-10-20 placed the blog index, articles and installation pages inside the gate. That text no longer applies.

## Required flow

1. A person requests a protected resource. Retain a sanitized allowlisted internal destination, including a legitimate article fragment. Never include survey answers, emails, tokens or arbitrary external URLs in return parameters.
2. Require real Google authentication through the website's existing provider. The server must validate the session and Google identity, not merely check a browser flag or decorative account cookie. No login skip is offered. An existing non-Google session does not automatically satisfy this policy.
3. After verified Google sign-in, show a short introduction if it has not been completed or skipped in the current authenticated session. Keep the requested resource title visible. Ask one useful question, such as What brings you to MindrianOS? Use only questions mapped to a defined decision and responsible team. Do not require a second email entry for the introduction.
4. Offer Continue to the requested page. Beneath it, use a discreet but readable Continue without answering link. Keep adequate contrast, focus visibility and keyboard/touch access. Only this introduction is skippable. No hidden or hover-only skip control.
5. Completing or skipping the introduction returns the authenticated person to the same requested resource. Skipping must work with empty or invalid fields, transmit no survey answers and create no marketing subscription. Partial answers stay in current-session memory until explicit submission.
6. A versioned introduction marker may suppress repeat prompts within the authenticated session. It stores only completed or skipped state, never answers or marketing permission. It is not an authentication credential. Storage failure permits authenticated intro skip, never anonymous content access. Clear it on sign-out and revalidate authentication on later requests.

## Authentication and interaction states

Signed out: show Sign in with Google and the actual access expectation. In progress: announce sign-in progress without claiming success. Cancelled or failed: explain how to retry and keep the requested resource protected. Callback accepted: validate identity and internal return destination before entering the introduction. Authenticated introduction: allow completion or skip without unnecessary validation. Submission failed: preserve answers and offer retry or authenticated continuation without sharing. Session expired: return to sign-in; intro progress never overrides expiry. Signed out again: protected requests require Google sign-in again.

A no-JavaScript path must use server-handled authentication and an authenticated skip action, not a public link that bypasses the login requirement. Protect the actual content/data response, not only navigation or a visual overlay. Server-rendered payloads, JSON/API routes, cached pages and prefetch must not expose the gated content to a signed-out visitor. OAuth tokens belong in the provider's secure session mechanism, never the intro marker, page URL, analytics or logs. Exact provider settings and route integration require website engineering review.

## Permission is independent

Google sign-in establishes identity for the requested access. It is not permission for marketing email, research participation, contact enrichment or a follow-up sequence. Explain the purpose of any introduction answers. Marketing permission remains an explicit separate unchecked choice and is not required for reading or installation. Reuse identity data only within reviewed purposes.

## Acceptance and evidence

Test signed-out navigation and direct links to the surfaces in scope, their content/data endpoints, spoofed markers, invalid or non-Google sessions, OAuth failure/cancellation, valid callback, external redirect rejection, session expiry and sign-out. Verify that blog, article, installation and documentation requests answer a signed-out visitor without sign-in. Verify both intro completion and skip reach the correct resource. Test skip with empty/invalid fields, disabled JavaScript, unavailable intro storage and failed submission. Test repeat-prompt suppression only within the intended authenticated session. Inspect actual network responses, caches, logs and replay configuration for content and personal-data leaks. Test keyboard, screen-reader feedback, reflow and focus at the login-to-intro transition.

Report login success, introduction completion, introduction skip, resource reached and subsequent participation separately. A skip is a legitimate choice. A login is not evidence of demand and does not establish marketing permission. Use only reviewed event properties and collection settings.

## CLI and update behavior

Relevant website, blog, documentation and form work receives this scoped active ruling through context, installed instruction snapshots and the review checklist. context.websiteAccess includes the actual policy and authority, not only a link. Other projects must check its named scope. Existing consumers update the private package, preview refresh, then apply refresh; old managed edits and historical runs remain protected. Doctor checks source consistency, not a deployed login or a completed security review.

Canonical machine-readable policy: policy.json. The old engagement/website-access.json is a supersession pointer, not an alternative access policy. The wider engagement proposal remains proposed.

## segments/experience/graph-visualization/entries/2026-10-21/GUIDE.md

# Graph visualization: show who sits next to whom

Use this segment when a project needs to show relationships between many things as a network: opportunities and the technologies inside them, ideas and their sources, people and projects. The reference build is the JHTV Opportunity Network (`https://jhtv-oliver-eureka-hub.vercel.app/network.html`, October 21, 2026). The files next to this guide are its working parts.

## 1. Earn the graph first

A network view is the right answer only when the question is about neighborhoods and bridges: what clusters together, what connects two clusters, and what stands alone. If the reader needs a ranking, use a list. If the reader needs a trend, use a chart. If there are fewer than about 15 nodes, draw a static diagram.

Ask one question in the page title, and make it the question the graph answers. The reference asks: "Who sits next to whom, and where might the next eureka be?"

## 2. Three kinds of node, three kinds of line

Keep the vocabulary small. Each mark has one meaning, taken from the color foundation.

| Mark | Meaning | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary object (for example, an opportunity) | A filed thing a person owns | Cobalt `#1F3FBF` sphere. Size grows with membership. Always labeled. |
| Newly added primary object | Filed today, still an assumption under challenge | Red `#D6281F` sphere, red label text. |
| Member (for example, a technology) | Content inside a primary object | Small sphere, tinted by neighborhood with muted hues that never compete with the primaries. |
| Bridge (possible eureka) | Not filed anywhere yet, but close to two or more neighborhoods | Yellow `#F2C200` sphere with an ink label chip that reads "Eureka? ID". |
| Filed relationship | A person linked these | Ink line, thin. |
| Engine relationship | An engine says the two descriptions mean similar things | Faint cobalt line. Thickness follows strength. Off by default below the engine's floor. |
| Bridge relationship | Crosses into another neighborhood | Thicker yellow line with slow red particles moving along it. This is the only animated mark. |

Relationship status still needs words. A 3D line cannot carry the dashed or dash-dot status lines from the color foundation, so the legend, the hover tip and the reading panel must say which kind each link is ("filed relationship", "engine link, similarity 0.62", "bridge link"). Never let color alone carry status.

Yellow is never text on paper. Use ink text on a yellow chip.

## 3. Neighborhoods and eurekas: how to compute them

1. **Similarity.** Reuse stored embeddings. Never re-encode texts pair by pair. Normalize each vector once, then take dot products. The reference compared about 2.27 million pairs of 384-dimension vectors in 34 seconds on one core at low priority. The room engine that re-encoded both texts for every pair ran for hours at full CPU and never finished. Run any batch job at the lowest priority (`nice -n 19`) and read the database read-only.
2. **Floors.** Use the engine's own thresholds and name them. The reference used 0.55 for a strong link (CONVERGES) and 0.45 for a weak one (INFORMS). Weak links are hidden by default.
3. **Neighborhoods.** Run weighted label propagation over the filed links (weight 1) and the strong engine links (weight = similarity). Sort ties by ID so the result is deterministic.
4. **Bridges.** For each item that is not yet filed, keep its best match in each neighborhood at or above the strong floor. If it reaches two or more neighborhoods, it is a bridge candidate. Rank by the sum of its two best scores and show the top group (the reference shows 16).

## 4. Check the data before anyone sees it

Similarity engines find copies as easily as insights. Before publishing, check for these failures:

- **Identical text.** Hash each record's description. Two records with the same hash will score near 1.0 and look like a discovery. In the reference, record C17716 carried a copy of C15551's text, and the resulting 0.948 "link" was removed.
- **Empty text.** Records with no description match on title alone. Count them and say so on the page.
- **Shared boilerplate.** Families of records that share template text form clusters that look tighter than they are. Label those links ("the two records share identical catalog text").
- **Every number sourced.** Spot-check any figure in generated plain-language text against its source record before publishing.

Put the result of the data check on the page, in a quiet panel. Readers trust a map more when they can see what was removed.

## 5. The reading panel is where the meaning lives

Clicking a node flies the camera to it and opens a panel. The panel turns the graph into a story. Use this content contract:

**Primary object**
- One-liner (14 words or fewer)
- How we found it: three to five sentences, told as a short story of what was looked at and what the method noticed
- What we found: two to three sentences
- What could kill it: one sentence, from the record's own risks
- Its members as clickable IDs, each with a one-line plain description
- A link to the full record

**Member or bridge**
- What it is, in plain words (40 words or fewer), written so a curious 15-year-old could follow it
- Who it might help (20 words or fewer)
- For a bridge: why it might be a eureka, and the neighborhoods it touches, with scores and links
- For a member: what it is part of, and what the engine says it is close to
- A link to the source record

Write in the Larry-meets-Feynman voice. Short sentences. An everyday comparison where it helps. Explain a method by what it did ("a scout read every record and noticed two that describe the same problem"), never by its internal acronym. Do not invent an expansion for an acronym the source does not define. Keep every hedge: these are candidates, not findings.

To write text for many nodes, split the nodes into batches of about 50 and give each batch to a separate writer with this contract. Check that the output parses, that it covers every ID, and that it contains no em dashes.

## 6. Interaction

- Drag to turn, scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag to pan. Click a node to read it. Click the background to clear.
- When a node is selected, dim everything outside its neighborhood and hide labels outside it.
- Persistent labels only for primary objects and bridges, projected from 3D to screen each frame. Members get a hover tip.
- Search accepts an ID, a title word or a word from the plain description.
- A "Show me the bridges" button tours each bridge in turn.
- Deep links: `network.html#ID` opens with that node selected.
- Optional slow rotation, off by default and disabled under reduced motion.

## 7. Accessibility and fallbacks

- The 3D stage carries an `aria-label` that points to the non-visual routes: search and the panel lists.
- If WebGL or the library fails, render the same content as a list of primary objects with their stories.
- The reading panel uses `aria-live="polite"`.
- On narrow screens the guide collapses and the reading panel becomes a bottom sheet.
- Minimum label size is 12 px. Mono labels use 0.04em tracking.

## 8. Build

- Library: `3d-force-graph`, pinned to an exact version at least two weeks old, loaded as one UMD script from jsDelivr. The reference uses `3d-force-graph@1.79.1`.
- Data travels as `network-data.js` (`window.NETWORK = {...}`), not fetched JSON, so the page also works when opened from disk inside a room.
- Forces: filed links short and strong, engine links longer and weak, bridge links longest and weakest, charge about -55. Call `zoomToFit` after the layout settles.
- The data shape is defined in `network-data.schema.json`. `derive-network.cjs` builds it read-only from a MindrianOS room database. `network.template.html` is the reference page.

## 9. Human check

Before publishing, confirm the following:

1. The title asks the question the graph answers.
2. Every color has one meaning, and the legend names it in words.
3. The data check ran, and its result is on the page.
4. Every node opens a panel with plain text and a source link.

# Visual explanation grammar

Active production ruling for Mindrian graphs, matrices, hierarchies, systems and framework explanations. Choose the relationship to explain before choosing a shape or animation.

## Learn the language from Theo Faces

The owner-supplied Theo Faces HTML is a construction and motion reference, not only an avatar gallery. Its planes are things under consideration. Its routes describe relationships or sequence. Its eyes direct attention. Its bar performs the thinking move: open, compare, descend, trace, challenge or shift. A gap keeps missing support visible. Colour names a role, not a framework or an automatic verdict.

Carry that grammar into the diagram itself. A graph can branch without being a face. A matrix can scan one cell without containing two eyes. A system can trace one possible return effect without anthropomorphising every node. Use the source framework glyph optionally as an introduction; keep the actual labels, values and reasoning in the full visual. The primary face / m:os logo stays fixed and separate.

## Choose the right structural contract

| Form | What its geometry means | Thinking move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| graph-data | Values on an explicit scale | Track or compare while axes stay fixed |
| graph-relationships | Named entities joined by typed relationships | Open one relevant branch |
| matrix-2x2 | Two independent distinctions and four combinations | Scan, focus, hold |
| pyramid | Declared support, hierarchy or source-defined levels | Open support or climb named levels |
| system | Boundary, components, influences, stocks/flows where appropriate | Trace one change and its possible return |
| framework | One subject examined through accurately named lenses | Shift perspective while keeping the subject |

Graphs of values and graphs of relationships are different contracts. Likewise an argument pyramid is not a knowledge ladder, and a closed path is not automatically causal feedback. Use the exact child recipe selected by the visual job.


## Geometry follows meaning

Critical: the explanation chooses the geometry. Mindrian colour, typography and identity do not require square nodes, straight connectors, right angles or sharp corners inside a diagram. React is the rendering tool; it is not a geometric style rule.

Use circles and arcs when rotation, recurrence, radial comparison or feedback is the relationship to explain. Use curves when they clarify continuity, influence or a route around labels. Use organic or porous contours when a scope is genuinely fuzzy, and preserve irregular domain boundaries when their shape carries meaning. Use straight axes, aligned grids and hard edges when scale, separation, hierarchy or a constraint needs them. Mixed geometry is valid when the parts mean different things.

Do not round everything as a new house style. Do not square an established diagram to resemble the logo. Preserve canonical layouts and essential distinctions. A curve alone does not establish feedback, and a soft boundary does not imply a probability distribution. State what the geometry means, keep labels clear, and make movement follow the chosen path. Geometry may change only when that transformation teaches something; decorative morphing is not an explanation.

Shaper drawing defaults are starting implementations. Choose a valid variant, extend the component or use a purpose-built view when the actual relationship needs a different form. The custom Shape primitive supports arbitrary SVG paths, a chosen viewBox, and explicit line caps and joins. Per-shape strokes remain overridable. InnovationTrinity offers straight or curved connections; FuturesWheel offers straight or curved links. These options do not change the framework's meaning or its evidence status.

## CSS options under the existing encoding

A new CSS capability may help carry an encoding that is already chosen: a separator for a group, a source kept beside its claim, evidence opened in place. It may not choose the encoding, create a relation the data lacks or replace the complete still. A corner, border shape or separator marks a role already defined in this language and never encodes status alone. Follow the [CSS capabilities room](../../../css-capabilities/entries/2026-10-23/GUIDE.md): semantic base first, feature test, off switch, fallback.

## Still first, one move, then hold

Establish the elements and readable labels. Perform one meaningful transformation. Settle with the relationship and open question available. The source uses 440 ms part movements, 700 ms eye/bar paths and at most 80 ms stagger with cubic-bezier(0.23,1,0.32,1). These are starting values for this source language, not universal reading durations or a reason to rush a larger explanation.

In full diagrams, keep essential type readable while shapes or attention change. Do not scale the words along with an emerging plane. Colour can arrive with the move, but no important distinction depends on colour alone. Preserve the roles of the person, Theo, project material and actual evidence. A focus shift is not a passed test; a line is not proof; a visited stage is not an executed operation.

Animations are finite. Optional replay repeats the same explanatory move. Reduced motion and disabled scripts retain a complete still. Longer reasoning needs labelled intermediate stills, not a blank substitute. A real scroll story uses the separate continuous-story directive and a shared reversible playhead. Do not force React onto a still chart or flatten a required interaction because a matching component is unavailable.

Encoding names what axes, nodes, levels, boundaries or lenses mean. motionMeaning says what the change teaches, or explicitly why the still is enough. stillState describes the complete non-moving explanation. The prepared record returns the selected recipe and actual instructions. Choosing a shape does not validate its factual content.

# The quest, uncertainty and changing conditions

**MindrianOS is a structured quest through an uncertain, changing problem space.** With Theo, you explore what might be possible, examine what would have to be true and choose a practical next move.

## A quest, not a route with a known destination

You can begin with a problem, a discovery or possible solution, or a business opportunity. A hypothesis can sit within any of these entrances. Work reveals more of the territory. Evidence changes your understanding, while needs, technologies, stakeholders and constraints may themselves change. Even the target can move.

The DataRoom holds the developing project and its relationships. Theo helps bring a relevant question into focus without losing the wider system. You retain judgment. The structure supports inquiry; it does not turn discovery into a guaranteed sequence of steps.

## Navigate uncertainty

Some exposures can be assessed from sufficiently understood outcomes and evidence. Other questions concern what the problem is, what could work, who might value it or which conditions matter. These need exploration and learning before a useful probability may be available. A project can contain both risk and uncertainty.

Ask what you know, what you assume, what conflicts and what could change your view. Turn a useful insight into a connection to investigate, an assumption to challenge or a test with a measurement and reconsideration condition. A neat score does not resolve an unknown.

## Avoid presentism

In this brand context, avoiding presentism means not treating today's needs, technologies, markets, business models or constraints as the permanent limits of what could matter. Current demand and current feasibility are evidence about present conditions, not complete answers about every possible future.

Ask: What could change? Which assumption depends on conditions staying the same? What would need to happen for this possibility to become useful? What signal would tell us to revisit it?

A future possibility is conditional, not a forecast presented as fact. Record the date and context of evidence, distinguish observed change from a scenario, and do not replace presentism with unsupported optimism. A weak current business case is something to understand, not automatically a reason to dismiss the inquiry or a license to ignore viability.

## Keep the triangle alive over time

Return to a problem worth solving, a solution that works and a business case that holds. Ask for whom, under which conditions and when. A possible change in one may alter the others. ## segments/writing/editorial-integrity/callouts/entries/2026-10-20/GUIDE.md

# Callouts: a clear reading job

Use a callout only when its role is different from surrounding prose. Keep one point, a short descriptive label and the relevant qualification. A box is emphasis, not evidence or a substitute for a visual explanation.

| Role | When to use it | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Note | Useful context that supports the main reading | Paper, ink text, blue inline-start rule, visible Note label |
| Limitation / Open question | What is not established or still needs examination | Paper, ink text, yellow inline-start rule, explicit status |
| Warning | A real consequence of an action, such as deleting stored work | Paper, ink text, red inline-start rule, consequence and recovery/prevention |
| Example | A concrete worked or fictional illustration | Neutral ink rule, Example label; disclose invented scenarios |
| Key point | An editorial summary written by Mindrian | Neutral inset; no quotation marks or invented speaker |

These labels and visual treatments are Mindrian house choices. They are not a new evidence-confidence score. Do not use warning styling to make marketing sound urgent. Do not use green Verified badges to turn a citation into proof.

## Placement and restraint

Place the callout beside the sentence or step it qualifies. Keep the main argument, prerequisites and essential consequences in the normal reading path as well. Never hide a destructive consequence in a small inset, a hover or a collapsed footnote. Avoid nested boxes and a callout after every paragraph. If everything needs highlighting, revise the structure.

For a research article, a limitation can keep the test conditions beside a finding. For documentation, put the expected result and any recovery step beside the command. For social, keep a material qualification on the same slide or in the immediate caption; a linked article cannot repair a misleading headline.

## Semantic implementation

Use an aside for genuinely supplementary material and a labelled section or ordinary block in the reading flow when the content is essential. Static warnings should not automatically receive role=alert. Use a visible label; color is supplementary. Do not use blockquote simply to indent a note.

Use logical CSS properties, selectable text, start alignment and no fixed-height clipping. Preserve lang and dir for Hebrew and isolate Latin commands or source names. The shared callouts.css is a starting point, not proof of contrast, reflow or screen-reader conformance. Test the actual page and obtain native Hebrew review where needed.

In Markdown, use the renderer's documented admonition syntax when it is supported. Otherwise use a plain labelled paragraph. Do not assume every platform renders a GitHub-style alert the same way.


## segments/writing/editorial-integrity/evidence/entries/2026-10-20/GUIDE.md

# Ground claims in what the sources support

Factuality is a publishing practice, not a tone of voice. Trace consequential factual statements, quotations, statistics and instructions to supporting material. Make it possible for another person to inspect the specific support and its limits.

## Classify before polishing

| Kind | What the reader must understand |
| --- | --- |
| Fact or reported finding | What was observed or reported, by whom, when and under which conditions |
| Inference | What Mindrian concludes from the evidence, and the reasoning connecting them |
| Hypothesis | What might be true and remains untested |
| Illustrative example | What is invented to explain a point |
| Technical instruction | Which version/environment was tested, the expected outcome and recovery |
| Quotation | The exact original wording, context and locator |

Do not upgrade a hypothesis through a confident heading, a solid animated connection, a statistic card or an attributed quote about something else. Distinguish evidence about current conditions from a possible future. Do not treat current absence of demand as permanent impossibility, or a plausible future as proof.

## Check a claim at its actual source

1. Write the specific publishable claim and its draft location. Open the original paper, official documentation, dataset, direct interview or recorded test when available. Search results, AI answers and repeated summaries are discovery aids.
2. Locate the passage, table, versioned behavior or test result that supports that exact wording. Record source identity, direct URL or internal locator, publication/version context, date checked and a short supporting extract or observation.
3. Read enough surrounding material to inspect conditions, limitations, scope and contradictory evidence. Distinguish preprints, author/vendor claims, peer-reviewed findings and your own tests. Prefer independent corroboration for contested or consequential claims; two pages repeating the same press release are not independent support.
4. For research, check the publisher's current record for corrections, expressions of concern or retraction when relevant. A DOI, citation count or Crossmark badge does not itself establish that a claim is sound.
5. Match the wording to the evidence. Preserve sample, denominator, unit, comparator, time window and uncertainty for numbers. Show calculation inputs for derived figures. Do not infer causation from a reported association or extrapolate a lab result to a business outcome without support.
6. Put a direct citation beside the claim, with a useful source title and locator. Keep important qualifications beside the claim too. Record unresolved conflicts and remove, qualify or hold statements that the available material does not support.

AI can help locate candidates, draft a paraphrase or compare supplied passages. It cannot create a missing reference or turn a source list into verification. Do not mark research checked when nobody inspected the original.

## Technical and product claims

Test the actual command or workflow on the stated package version and environment. Record the command, expected and observed result, date and relevant limits. An official API page supports documented behavior, not proof that your integration works. A prototype screenshot does not establish a shipped capability. Separate current functionality, examples, proposals and roadmap plans.

## Channel handoff and corrections

Blog: keep source links close, explain method/scope and show interpretation separately. Documentation: keep version, prerequisites, expected result and recovery in the task flow. Social: retain the qualifier, correct number context and readable attribution in the post or immediate caption; provide a source link rather than a disconnected bibliography.

For a material error, correct the published content and affected derivatives, record what changed and when, and tell readers when the correction changes the interpretation. Do not silently replace a claim with a different one and retain the old review record.

## Optional evidence ledger

Use ledger-template.json to record declared claims and sources. Bind it to the exact draft SHA-256. `mos-brand review --file draft.md --evidence ledger.json` checks this binding, required fields, source links between declared records, claim presence and literal quote matches against supplied extracts. It does not fetch sources, authenticate a reviewer, verify source truth or discover every unlisted claim. A passing structure always leaves human review pending.

Keep private research, interview identities and restricted source extracts in the team's work record. Publish only citations and material you may share. The CLI never publishes the ledger automatically.


## segments/writing/editorial-integrity/quotations/entries/2026-10-20/GUIDE.md

# Quotations: exact words, visible context

A quote box signals that real words came from a real source. Verify the original passage before styling it. Preserve meaning, wording, qualifications and the circumstances that affect its interpretation. AI-generated text and a remembered quotation are leads to check, not original sources.

## Identify what you are publishing

- Direct quote: exact source wording, with visible speaker or institution, source title, date when relevant, direct link and page, section or timestamp.
- Paraphrase: your faithful restatement, with a citation and no quotation marks.
- Translation: visibly identify it as a translation and retain the original source and passage for bilingual review. Do not imply word-for-word identity with the original language.
- Key point or pull statement: your own summary, labelled as editorial emphasis. Do not attribute it to an outside person.

Do not invent customer testimonials, Larry or professor quotations, endorsements, interview participants or source details. Mindrian's presenter voice does not make generated wording a real quotation from Prof. Aronhime. Use a traceable transcript and its actual locator for his direct words.

## Edit without changing the claim

Choose a short, relevant passage. Do not splice separate statements into a stronger claim. Mark omissions and editorial insertions visibly, and preserve the surrounding context in the work record. If the excerpt cannot be accurate and readable, paraphrase and cite it instead.

The brand never uses em dashes. If the original passage contains one, choose another exact excerpt or use a clearly identified paraphrase. Do not silently replace source punctuation and keep calling the text verbatim. Keep excerpts proportionate to the purpose and check applicable reuse terms; this book grants no permission to republish long extracts.

## Display

Use blockquote for the actual quotation, within a figure when attribution is in a figcaption. Use the cite attribute for its source URL if useful, and provide a visible source link too. The attribute alone is not readable attribution. Keep the person or institution in ordinary text; use cite for the title of the cited work. Do not use huge decorative quote marks, portrait authority cues or tiny source text to disguise weak support.

For social cards, include readable attribution and a source route in the card/caption. The selected words must retain the original scope even when viewed without the rest of the carousel. A visually impressive quote is not evidence for a different claim beside it.
